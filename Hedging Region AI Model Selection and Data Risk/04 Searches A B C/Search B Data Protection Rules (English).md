# Data-Protection Rules by Jurisdiction (Search B)

Oct 16, 2026 · @Ian

## What this answers

This table shows, for Hong Kong, mainland China, Singapore and Taiwan, what the law says about sending personal data to an AI provider abroad. It supports the "data-control needs" half of Q1 and feeds Q5. Mainland China has the strictest rule, Hong Kong has no statutory limit in force, Singapore requires comparable protection abroad, and Taiwan's transfer rule is moving to a new regulator. A Traditional Chinese version is in the second tab. This is a research summary, not legal advice.

## Coding scheme

- **status**: in_force, not_in_operation (law text exists but the commencement date has never been set), or passed_not_effective (enacted, effective date still to be set).

- **source_type**: primary_law (official statute site), regulator_page, law_firm_summary, or unofficial_translation. Law-firm summaries are second-hand and are marked as such.

- **read vs not read**: a page counts as read only if I opened it and its text came back. Pages that returned empty or were blocked are listed separately in Caveats.

- **Dates**: date shown on the source where there is one, plus my viewing date 2026-10-16.

## Checks an organisation can ask before choosing a provider

These are questions, not legal conclusions. They connect the rules above to the provider table in Search A.

1.  **Does the prompt contain personal data?** If staff only send non-personal text, the transfer rules in this table mostly do not apply. If they send customer or staff data, they do.

2.  **Which place's law governs the data?** Mainland data faces the PIPL volume thresholds. Hong Kong has no transfer limit in force but the PCPD expects due diligence. Singapore needs comparable protection abroad. Taiwan's rule is changing.

3.  **Where does the provider store and process it?** Use the storage column in Search A. A Singapore server is a storage fact, not a legal-jurisdiction fact.

4.  **What does the contract say about training and retention?** Use the training and retention columns in Search A. A contract can close a gap that the law leaves open.

5.  **Is there a written internal policy?** The PCPD checklist asks organisations to say which AI tools are allowed and what data may go in.

## Caveats

- **Most sources here are law-firm summaries.** Only Singapore section 26 and the China Law Translate PIPL text are close to the law itself, and the PIPL text is an unofficial crowdsourced translation with no date shown.

- **PCPD pages could not be read.** The PCPD AI page, the 31 March 2025 press release and the guidance list returned empty text through my page reader. I did not try another way around that. Their addresses are in the reference list as found-not-read.

- **Two official sites blocked reading.** Taiwan's law database and Singapore's PDPC site refuse automated readers, so I did not read them. Taiwan Article 21's wording and the PDPC guideline text are therefore not verified from the source.

- **Blog claims are weaker.** The UPPERSETUP post is an individual's blog. Use it only for the 2026 "no bill on section 33" point and confirm with the PCPD.

- **Not covered.** China's generative AI service rules, Taiwan's financial-sector rules, sector rules for health, Macau, and penalty detail for Singapore. Hong Kong, Singapore and Australia bank-regulator pages are in the section above and are only the pages Gemini Round 3 pointed us to.

- **Dates move.** Taiwan's effective date was still open on 13 January 2026. Check again before submission.
